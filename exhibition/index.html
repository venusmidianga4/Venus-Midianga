Glitching the Self

Digital Identity, Performance, and Fragmentation

Exhibition curated by Venus Midianga

Curatorial Text

This exhibition, Glitching the Self, brings together a group of net-based artworks that explore how identity is constructed, performed, and destabilized within digital environments. Spanning works by Shawne Michaelain Holloway, Auriea Harvey, Martine Syms, and the project Keeping Up Appearances, these pieces collectively examine the internet not just as a platform, but as a space where the self becomes fragmented, multiplied, and mediated through screens, avatars, and interfaces. A key throughline across these works is the use of digital performance as a strategy for interrogating identity. Holloway’s work stages the body within virtual environments, often blurring boundaries between intimacy and alienation. Similarly, Auriea Harvey’s Skinonskinonskin uses 3D graphics and interactive storytelling to evoke a tactile yet disembodied sense of presence. Martine Syms’ work engages with the aesthetics of the web itself—hyperlinks, scrolling, and fragmented text—to reflect on cultural identity within digital space. Meanwhile, Keeping Up Appearances critiques the construction of online personas through curated self-presentation. Together, these works align with post-internet art practices, using glitch, repetition, and fragmentation to reflect the instability of identity in digital culture.
